**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

## Castalia OS turns the PC you retired into the one you reach for — an original, XP-class desktop that boots happily in 512 MB of RAM

*Independent studio Tombatossals Softworks unveils "Human," the warm flagship
edition of a from-scratch desktop operating system built for the millions of
early- and late-2000s computers the modern web left behind — with no Microsoft
code, branding or artwork anywhere in it.*

**July 2026 —** There is a drawer, a closet, a school store-room in every
country full of Pentium 4s, Core 2 Duos and Athlons that still power on and
still have nothing useful to run. Modern operating systems treat them as
e-waste. **Castalia OS** treats them as the target.

Today Tombatossals Softworks is showing **Castalia OS "Human"**, the flagship
edition of an independent desktop operating system written from the ground up
for machines with as little as **512 MB of RAM** and a 20-year-old CPU — and
built to feel, on that hardware, *fast*.

Castalia OS is not a theme pack and not a Windows clone. It is a curated Linux
distribution with its **own desktop shell**, **46 first-party applications**,
a **seven-theme design system** and a **managed compatibility layer** for
old Windows, DOS and ScummVM software — every pixel of it original, and not a
line of Microsoft code or a byte of Microsoft artwork anywhere inside.

### Familiar, but ours

The "Human" edition wears a warm, mid-2000s look on purpose: chocolate
titlebars, sun-orange accents, a hand-drawn "Human Dawn" wallpaper and a
family of 39 tangerine icons. It is instantly legible to anyone who used a
computer in that decade — and entirely Castalia's own design, down to a
provenance ledger that the project's continuous integration refuses to let
anyone violate.

Underneath the warmth is discipline. **One theme file** drives the whole
system — the Qt controls, the window decorations, the icons, the login
greeter and the sounds all change together, and automated checks verify every
theme for colour contrast (including a WCAG-AAA High Contrast mode) and for
"16-bit-safe" gradients that won't band on the era's graphics chips. The
desktop shell is **event-driven rather than polling**, the taskbar updates the
instant a window opens, and animations are short, optional and switch off
entirely on request or on the lowest-end machines.

### A real desktop, with real applications

Castalia OS boots a live ISO to a genuine graphical desktop under a real
window manager — verified in emulation, not mocked up — and it comes with a
full suite of native applications: a file manager, a rich-text editor, a
paint program, a calculator, an image viewer, a terminal with its own VT100
emulator, sticky notes, a clock, a magnifier, Minesweeper- and
Solitaire-class games, a system monitor, a hardware **benchmark and
diagnostics** tool, a **Software Center**, an **Update Center** that quietly
takes a Restore Point before it changes anything, and a **Recovery Center**
built on a unit-tested Restore Points engine. A guided graphical installer —
with a text-mode fallback — puts it on a disk, and refuses every destructive
step until you type the target drive to confirm it.

For the software people can't leave behind, a managed compatibility layer runs
original Windows programs under Wine in per-application sandboxes, and a
Classic Games launcher brings up MS-DOS titles under DOSBox-X and adventure
games under ScummVM.

### Built to be trusted with an old machine

Because these are often someone's *only* computer, Castalia OS is built to be
recoverable: Restore Points, an independent recovery environment and a
safe-mode path mean an update or a mistake is never the end of the story.

### Open, and looking for its first reviewers

Castalia OS is a working developer preview, version 0.1.0. Its source code,
its 23-section design "bible" and its entire build-and-test pipeline are open.
The team is now inviting press, reviewers and the retro-computing and
Linux communities to take a first look — builds, live demos and review
imagery at any resolution are available on request.

> "We didn't want to make a museum piece or a nostalgia skin," say creators
> **Dave Abellán** and **Claudio di Castello**. "We wanted the computer your
> family gave up on to boot in a few seconds, look beautiful, and do real work
> — and to be *ours*, honestly built, with nothing borrowed that wasn't given
> freely. Castalia is the desktop we wished those machines had shipped with."
